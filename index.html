My promise to you

The rules I work by — in writing.

You're being asked to trust someone with personal things. You deserve to know exactly where the lines are before you decide, so here they are. These are in the agreement too, not just on this page.

I never touch your money

Never your bank, your investments, your retirement accounts, your taxes, Social Security or Medicare. I won't ask for those passwords and I won't accept them if offered. I'll happily sit with you while you log in — that's different.

Your passwords stay yours

They live in a locked app in your name, paid for by you, that you control. If you ever stop working with me, you keep everything and I keep nothing. No hostage-taking.

Someone in your family always knows

Every client names a family member at the start — that's required, not optional. They get a written summary every month of every account I touched and everything I changed. I'd rather be watched.

You watch me work

When I need to be in one of your accounts, we do it together on a screen share while you watch. I don't log into your things when you're not there.

I'll never cold-call you

I won't phone or email out of the blue saying there's a problem and I need access right now. That's what the scammers do. If you get that call, it isn't me — hang up and call me back on the number on this page.

If something's wrong, I say so

If I think you're being scammed, I tell you and your family contact the same day, and I'll point you to your bank's fraud line and to Connecticut Protective Services for the Elderly at 1-888-385-4225.

About me

I'm somebody's grandson, too.

The name is a joke, but it's also the honest description. Everyone who has a young relative who's "good with computers" ends up leaning on them — and everyone who doesn't have one is stuck. I'd like to be that person for a handful of families around Fairfield County.

I build software for a living, and before this I worked at a digital privacy firm called 360 Privacy. My job there was building the automated systems that hunt down people's personal information and get it taken off the internet — the data-broker sites quietly selling your address, your phone number, your age, and the names of your children to anyone willing to pay a few dollars.

That work taught me the thing most people never find out until it's too late: scams aren't guesswork any more. When someone calls claiming to be your grandson in trouble and knows his name, your town, and where you go to church, they didn't guess. They bought it. That's the side of it I worked on — taking the information back down.

The people paying for that kind of protection could afford it. The scams aimed at everybody else use exactly the same information — so this is the same work, at a price a normal household can actually pay.

I'm not a call center and I'm not a franchise. You'll have my phone number, and I'm the one who answers it. I take on a small number of clients on purpose, because the whole point is that you're not a ticket in a queue.
